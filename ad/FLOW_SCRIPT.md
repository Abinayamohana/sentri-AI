# SentriAI — Ad Film Script (Google Flow opening + screen recording)

Structure of the finished video:

| Block | Source | Runtime |
|---|---|---|
| **Act 1 — the problem** | Google Flow (7 clips × 8s) | 0:00 – 0:56 |
| **Act 2 — the product** | `ad/1000062389.mp4` (screen recording, 2:14 full) | 0:56 – ~2:10 |
| **Act 3 — the payoff** | Google Flow (2 clips × 8s) | ~2:10 – 2:26 |

Two cuts are described at the end: **Full (~2:25)** and **Short (~1:30)** for social.

---

## Before you generate anything: the three lock blocks

Flow drifts between clips. Paste these **verbatim** into every prompt — same words, same order.
Generate one clip first, then use **Frames to Video / Ingredients** with a still of Kamala from
clip 1 as the character reference for clips 2–7 and 9. That is what actually holds the face.

**CHARACTER — KAMALA**
> A 78-year-old South Indian woman, short and slight, soft round face with deep smile lines, warm
> brown skin, silver hair in a small low bun, thin gold-rimmed glasses, small gold stud earrings, a
> faded teal cotton saree with a thin maroon border, one thin gold bangle on each wrist. Slow,
> unhurried movements.

**CHARACTER — RAVI**
> A 46-year-old South Indian man, 46, close-cropped black hair greying at the temples, short beard,
> pale blue linen shirt with sleeves rolled up, tired eyes.

**SET**
> A modest South Indian ground-floor home. Red oxide floor, pale green wall with peeling paint, a
> wooden armchair with a faded cushion, a steel tumbler of tea, a small wooden side table with a
> black smartphone standing upright in a stand, a window with white lace curtains.

**STYLE (append to every prompt)**
> Shot on 35mm film, Kodak Portra colour, shallow depth of field, natural light only, handheld with
> a slight breathing motion, cinematic documentary realism. No text, no subtitles, no captions, no
> logos, no on-screen graphics.

Two rules that save you re-rolls:
- **Don't ask Flow for spoken narration.** Generate ambient audio only, and lay the voiceover in
  the edit — you get clean, consistent narration instead of seven different AI voices. The one
  exception is Scene 7, where the phone's line must be in-scene.
- **Never prompt an impact.** "She falls" gets refused or looks fake. Scene 3 shows the *aftermath*
  — a dropped stick, a rolling tumbler, an empty chair. It's also better filmmaking.

# ACT 1 — The problem (Google Flow, 7 clips)

---

### Scene 1 · 0:00 – 0:08 · The silence

**Flow prompt**
> [SET] Static wide shot. [KAMALA] sits alone in the wooden armchair by the window, holding a steel
> tumbler of tea in both hands, looking at nothing. Mid-morning sunlight through lace curtains
> throws a slow-moving pattern of light across the red floor. Nothing in the room moves except dust
> in the sunbeam. She takes one slow sip and sets the tumbler down. Ambient audio only: a ceiling
> fan turning, a distant crow, a clock ticking. No music, no speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover (lay in edit)**
> Kamala is seventy-eight. She lives alone.

---

### Scene 2 · 0:08 – 0:16 · Nobody asks

**Flow prompt**
> [SET] Slow push-in on a refrigerator door. Taped to it, a handwritten medicine chart on lined
> paper, curling at one corner. Below it on the counter, three blister strips of tablets — one
> half-used, two untouched. [KAMALA] enters frame from the left, out of focus behind the strips,
> pauses, looks at the chart for a moment, then walks past it out of frame without taking anything.
> Ambient audio only: a fridge hum, slippers on a stone floor. No music, no speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover**
> Four medicines. Three different times. Two of them tied to meals. Nobody asks whether she took
> them — not until the next appointment.

---

### Scene 3 · 0:16 – 0:24 · Nobody notices

**Flow prompt**
> [SET] Low static shot at floor level, camera resting on the red oxide floor. A wooden walking
> stick lies fallen across the frame. A steel tumbler rolls slowly to a stop, spilled tea spreading
> in a thin line. The armchair above is empty, its cushion pushed askew. In the soft background, out
> of focus and far away on the side table, a black smartphone stands upright, screen dark. Nothing
> else moves. Ambient audio only: the tumbler settling, the ceiling fan, then silence. No music, no
> speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover**
> *(let the tumbler settle, then — )* Ten in the morning.

---

### Scene 4 · 0:24 – 0:32 · The hours

**Flow prompt**
> [SET] Locked-off shot of the pale green wall and the side table. A time-lapse of daylight crossing
> the wall: a hard bright rectangle of sun stretches, softens, turns orange, then blue as evening
> comes. The smartphone on the table lights up, buzzes, goes dark. Lights up, buzzes, goes dark
> again. The room is empty and still. Ambient audio only: a phone vibrating against wood, birds
> outside changing from day to evening. No music, no speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover**
> Six in the evening. Somebody rings, gets no answer, and only then starts to worry.

---

### Scene 5 · 0:32 – 0:40 · Two hours away

**Flow prompt**
> [RAVI] sits in a parked car at dusk, lit blue by the phone screen in his hand, a call ringing
> unanswered on speaker. He looks out through the windscreen at nothing, jaw tight, then drops the
> phone into the passenger seat and starts the engine. Close handheld shot from the passenger side.
> Ambient audio only: an unanswered ringtone, rain on the roof, an engine turning over. No music, no
> speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover**
> Her son lives two hours away. He would rather be woken by a false alarm than by silence.

---

### Scene 6 · 0:40 – 0:48 · The two bad answers

**Flow prompt**
> Two-part shot in one take. First: an extreme close-up of a medical alert pendant lying unworn on
> a bedside table beside a glass of water, its cord coiled, a thin layer of dust on it. The camera
> drifts right and racks focus to reveal, in the corner of the room, a small white security camera
> on a shelf with a steady red recording light, pointed straight at the empty armchair. The red
> light is the brightest thing in the frame. Ambient audio only: room tone, a faint electronic hum.
> No music, no speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover**
> A pendant only works if she is wearing it, awake, and able to press it — the three things least
> likely to be true. And a camera that streams her home to somebody's server is not a thing most
> families will accept. It shouldn't have to be the trade.

---

### Scene 7 · 0:48 – 0:56 · The turn

This is the only clip where you want Flow's native audio dialogue. Put the line in quotes exactly
as written; the phone speaks, not a person.

**Flow prompt**
> [SET] Same low floor-level angle as before. The fallen walking stick is still in frame. On the
> side table, the black smartphone screen wakes on its own — a soft warm amber glow spreading across
> the dark red floor toward the camera. A calm, warm woman's voice comes from the phone speaker,
> unhurried and gentle: "Kamala? I heard something. I've told Ravi — he's on his way. I'm staying
> right here with you." As it speaks, a hand with thin gold bangles slowly enters the bottom of the
> frame and rests flat on the floor, turning toward the light. Slow push-in on the glowing phone.
> Ambient audio: a quiet room, the ceiling fan, the voice from the small speaker. No music. [STYLE]

**Voiceover** — *none. Let the phone's line land in silence.*

---

### The bridge into the screen recording (0:56)

Hold on the glowing phone screen for about half a second past the end of Scene 7, then **match cut**
straight to the first frame of `ad/1000062389.mp4` — the same rectangle of light becomes the actual
app. One line of voiceover over the cut:

> This is SentriAI.

# ACT 3 — The payoff (Google Flow, 2 clips)

---

### Scene 8 · ~2:10 – 2:18 · The alert arrives

**Flow prompt**
> Close-up of [RAVI]'s hand holding a phone at night, screen bright in the dark. He is mid-stride,
> walking fast. The camera stays tight on his face as he reads, then he breaks into a run out of the
> right of frame, leaving the shot empty on a wet street under a sodium streetlight. Handheld,

**Voiceover**
> The message names what happened, the words she actually said, and how sure it was.

### Scene 9 · ~2:18 – 2:26 · The point

**Flow prompt**
> [SET] Warm lamplight, later that night. [KAMALA] is back in the wooden armchair with a blanket
> over her knees, tired but calm. [RAVI] crouches beside the chair holding her hand in both of his,
> his back to the camera. Neither speaks. On the side table beside them the black smartphone sits
> upright in its stand, screen dark now. Slow, almost imperceptible push-in. Hold the last two
> seconds completely still. Ambient audio only: a ceiling fan, rain easing off outside. No music, no
> speech. [STYLE]

**Voiceover**
> Between the moment something happens and the moment help arrives, there are minutes with nobody in
> them. An SMS can't fill those.
>
> A voice can.

**End card** (add in the editor, not in Flow — Flow renders text badly):
> **SentriAI**
> A phone that watches a room when nobody else can — and, when it matters, talks.

# Production notes

- **Aspect ratio.** Generate Act 1 and Act 3 in the same ratio as the screen recording. The capture
  is a portrait phone screen, so 16:9 will letterbox it. Either generate the Flow clips at 16:9 and
  place the phone capture in a device frame on a blurred backdrop, or generate everything 9:16 and
  ship vertical. **9:16 is the better call** — the whole film is about a phone.
- **Colour.** Grade Act 1 cool and desaturated, and let Act 2 and Act 3 come back to warm. The
  turn happens at Scene 7's amber glow; that should be the first warm frame in the film.
- **Music.** None until Scene 7. Bring in a single sustained piano or strings note under the phone's
  line, hold it through Act 2 low under the device audio, and resolve it on Scene 9. Silence in Act
  1 is the point — don't score the loneliness, just let it be quiet.
- **Device audio.** In Act 2, never talk over the app's own voice. Every line in the table above
  goes in a gap, not on top.
- **Re-rolling.** If a clip drifts on Kamala's face, don't re-prompt from scratch — regenerate from
  the Scene 1 reference frame. If Scene 7's spoken line comes out wrong, generate it silent and
  drop a recorded line in the edit; it's the safest clip to fix in post.
